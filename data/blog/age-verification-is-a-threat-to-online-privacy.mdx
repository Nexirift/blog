In March of 2023, the eSafety commissioner submitted a roadmap document on age verification to the Australian government. This document contained information on the motivation behind the propsal and how age verification should be implemented to "prevent harm to children from online pornography." (eSafety Commissioner, n.d.).

Later that year, in August of 2023, the Australian government released a response to the roadmap submitted by the eSafety commissioner (Australian Infrastructure, 2023). They approved of the document and have started work on the "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Bill 2024" (Parliament of Australia, 2024).

Both, the eSafety commissioner and the Australian government are using the classic excuse of "protecting the children" to justify this age verification proposal. This is not a good enough excuse and seems poor on the part of the government, who's main goal could possibly be censorship or to control the media that citizens view.

Ignoring the fact that age verification could possibly be used to censor content and media online, it also has the potential to have massive security implications. So far, it seems like the government has no idea what they're doing! It sounds like they want the individual platforms to implement this verification.

If the government *really* wanted to "protect the children", then they would be handling the verification data themselves. But, even then, there is still a huge risk of data being leaked via data breaches or the somewhere along the chain being compromised like a server hosting the script files that websites need to use.

If you are an Australian citizen, I suggest that you find alternative ways to access social media platforms, including "visiting" a different country who doesn't have these laws in place.
